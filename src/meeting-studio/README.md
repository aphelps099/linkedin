# Meeting studio

The canonical `/studio/` is a charcoal video-meeting window inside the cobalt racing-team website, with black navigation, a thin racing banner, small mic/name badges, green active-speaker borders, Gallery/Speaker view menu, and a familiar bottom meeting toolbar. The music progress strip is inside the meeting window. `/studio-2/` retains the giant ASCII performance stage.

The room uses the existing Studio 02 score, rendered audio packs, Web Audio scheduler, and playlist. It never asks for a camera or microphone and opens no real conference connection. Playback starts only through a user gesture. Clicking an idle participant starts the meeting; after playback begins, clicking a participant spotlights it. The same keyed portrait canvases and robot video stay mounted during layout changes. Speaker view follows the scheduled male/female voice and holds the last speaker between phrases. Grid view and Escape clear the spotlight. The Mix panel includes opt-in automatic spotlight direction, beat energy, confidence, voice mode, vocabulary pairs and chorus recall.

Mic buttons change actual audio gain: Strategic Partnerships = male voice plus drums; People & Culture = female voice plus keys; Workflow Automation = synth/lift layer; Thought Leader = bass. The meeting enables `meetingVoices` on the shared engine; the ASCII studio keeps its original routing. Muting uses short gain ramps while sources keep running. Mutes survive pause, seeking and song changes. Reset restores all parts. Captions and the green border follow the audible scheduled speaker, not instrument rotation. Camera-off “Connecting to audio…” remains the running joke.

Humans use still portraits with four stepped mouth poses and independently timed eyelid blinks. The background, body and head are fixed. `scripts/build-face-atlases.py` extracts five frames per person from the approved 10-second Runway renders (task IDs in `webcam-briefs.json`). WebP atlases contain the untouched frames; `portrait-renderer.js` blends only small mouth and eyelid regions into the neutral base. These are deliberately simple amplitude-driven poses, not phonetic lip sync. No per-song video generation is required.

The audio engine caches 50 Hz envelopes from the actual decoded isolated vocal samples. The same scheduled cue determines speaker, phrase start, seek offset, and mouth amplitude. `performanceFrame()` follows the audio clock and output timestamp where supported. Silence, pause, mute, instrumental mode and canceled loads close the mouth. Vocabulary changes, confidence, chorus recalls and track changes use their scheduled audio cues. Rendering caps at 24 fps, skips unchanged poses, and stops offscreen, in hidden tabs, with reduced motion or when the camera is off. Portraits are visible before playback; no human MP4 is downloaded. Stop video restores the avatar treatment, and Pause motion leaves neutral expressions.

The copier retains its silent 9.5-second MP4 loop; it is an original rectangular copier appliance design. The human MP4s remain in the asset directory as source references but are no longer consumed by the room. Existing audio packs and all six songs are retained.

Verification: `node scripts/check-face-performance.mjs`, `node scripts/check-studio2.mjs`, `node scripts/check-meeting.mjs`, and `npm run build`. The face test uses real decoded vocal samples and covers silence, turn handoffs, independent speaker mutes, pause/resume, seek, delivery changes, vocabulary and track changes. The actual canvas renderer was inspected through native-canvas contact sheets of all mouth/blink poses. The meeting check exercises participant gain routing, persistent mute state, silence/reset, director decisions, and server-rendered controls/assets. Media decoded successfully with ffmpeg and posters were visually inspected. Browser control was unavailable, so real-browser visual and audible QA remains outstanding.
