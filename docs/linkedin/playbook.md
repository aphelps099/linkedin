# LinkedIn Beats: company page playbook

Form LB-PB · Rev. 2026-10 · For internal thought leadership only

Page: https://www.linkedin.com/company/linked-beats/
Site: https://linkedinbeats.com
Owner: Aaron Phelps. Published posts are the record; this file is the operating manual.

This playbook extends the project bible (`docs/project/`, when merged). Where the two disagree, the bible's writing rules win and this file's publishing rules win.

---

## 1. The premise

Linked Beats is a company that exists to announce things. The page is that company's actual LinkedIn page, run with total sincerity. Nobody on the page knows it is funny. The site does the winking; the page never does.

The joke is the gap between the language, the resources committed, and the modest real outcome. The craft is in never pointing at the gap.

One line carries the whole tone, and it goes in the About section: "Form CB-16. For internal thought leadership only."

## 2. Who speaks

The cast speaks through the page, never through separate accounts. Fake employee profiles violate LinkedIn's user agreement and are the fastest way to lose the page, the audience, and Aaron's own profile in one enforcement action. The show lives inside the posts.

| Mechanism | How it works |
| --- | --- |
| Byline | Every post closes with a role credit: "People & Culture, Linked Beats." Readers learn the cast through voice. |
| Dialogue posts | Four to six lines of meeting transcript. The best material is already exchanges. |
| Page comments on itself | The company page replies once under its own post, prefixed with a role. "Strategy: Let's zoom out on this one." |
| Clips and art | Faces live on video and in the office-paper art, not in profile photos. |
| The one human | Aaron's real account comments, reshares, and tells the making-of story. This is the honest layer and the most LinkedIn-native one. |

The roster, with each character's established function (see `docs/project/characters.md` for the full sheet):

- **Synergy** (Host). Wants every meeting to end in alignment.
- **Synthia** (Corporate training). Makes process teachable, then trains people on the training.
- **People & Culture**. Solves exclusion by adding a committee.
- **Strategy**. Widens scope until the original question no longer applies.
- **Kevin** (Partnerships). Still cannot access the shared folder.
- **Keys** (Enablement). Builds helpful systems that create new dependencies.
- **Operations**. Takes authentic pride in a folder rename.
- **Arjun** (Finance). Asks the plain question. The audience's proxy. Never snarky.
- **Linda** (Governance). Remembers the last rollout and still has the spreadsheet.
- **Pat** (Thought leadership). Publicly present, camera off, absent when needed.
- **The printer** and **the floor droid**. Silent. They do the actual work.

## 3. The grid: genre times topic

Two lists. A post is one cell.

**Seven LinkedIn genres** (the shape of the announcement):

1. **The tool confession.** "I used ChatGPT to ___." Give the tool a specific, modest job. The character still checks and applies the result.
2. **The humbling.** "Humbled to be recognized at ___." Lanyard photo energy. The award is never named.
3. **The unexpected lesson.** "What my mom's birthday taught me about sales." Personal life harvested for a takeaway. The life underneath is real and fine.
4. **The rejection reframe.** "I was passed over. Here's why I'm grateful."
5. **The vulnerable share.** "I cried after a 1:1 today. And that's okay."
6. **The anti-hustle hustle.** "I took Friday off. My output went up."
7. **The announcement.** "Thrilled to share I'm starting a new chapter." The chapter is the same job.

**Six realistic topics** (the credible work underneath, from the bible's standing rule of October 29, 2026):

1. Onboarding
2. Google Drive sharing
3. Website updates
4. A social media intern's post getting impressions
5. Time spent on professional development
6. Conference follow-up

Start with a credible piece of work and its real outcome. Inflate only the significance and the ceremony. Never invent impact: impressions are not people, training is not mastery, a follow-up email is not a partnership.

## 4. The dry mechanic

Extremely dry means: a character reads their post aloud in a meeting, and colleagues take it at face value. Nobody reacts. Arjun asks the one question LinkedIn never asks. The scene ends when someone converts the question into process. No sting, no button, no caption explaining it.

Calibration samples:

> Keys: "I used ChatGPT to draft a guide to our shared Drive."
> People & Culture: "Love this."
> Kevin: "Does the guide say how to get access?"
> Keys: "It says to ask your manager."
> Kevin: "Who is my manager?"
> Synergy: "Let's take that offline."

> Strategy: "Humbled to be recognized at Alignment Summit 2026."
> Arjun: "What was the award?"
> Strategy: "Recognition."
> Arjun: "For what?"
> Strategy: "Attendance was a component."
> Linda: "We received one in 2019. It's in the spreadsheet."

> Operations: "My mom turned 68 on Saturday. Here's what it taught me about sales."
> Arjun: "What did it teach you?"
> Operations: "That relationships matter."
> Arjun: "Did you sell anything?"
> Operations: "I brought the cake."
> Synergy: "Let's capture that as a learning."

**Writing rules**

- The post is sincere and the colleague is literal. The gap does the work.
- One modest, specific outcome per post. "We combined two steps" beats "transformed how we work."
- No reaction faces, no winks, no parade of buzzwords. One or two corporate phrases, placed precisely.
- Punch at the format and the pressure to announce, never at a junior person, a mother, or anyone's competence.
- Characters stay consistent with the roster. Arjun is not a snark machine. Linda retains information, not just cynicism.
- No hashtags. No emoji. No "Agree?" The format is already the joke.
- A plain door at the end, one line, no pitch: "Beat available in the Cutting Room." Or nothing.

## 5. Five formats

Each format maps to an asset the project already owns.

| Format | What it is | Source asset | Cadence |
| --- | --- | --- | --- |
| The Achievement | Text post, one character, one topic, one genre, byline | The grid | 3 per week |
| The Transcript | Four to six lines of meeting dialogue | Bible scenes, meeting creator | 1 per week |
| The Clip | 30 to 45 second native video, captions burned in, posted as internal culture | Cutting Room films | 1 per week |
| The Credential | Image post congratulating a character on a certificate | Learn certificates, future Roast certificate | 1 every 2 weeks |
| The Document | PDF document post in the Swiss form language, four to six pages, almost no words | Design handoff system | 1 every 2 weeks |

LinkedIn's API supports organic text, image, video, article link and PDF document posts. Organic multi-image carousels are not supported by API and must be posted by hand.

## 6. Cadence and timing

- One post per weekday for the first three weeks, then settle at four per week.
- Alternate two slots: 08:15 and 12:10 Pacific. Odd minutes read human. Nothing posts at :00.
- Never two tool confessions in a row. Never the same character two days running.
- Page replies to its own post once, in character, within the first hour. Aaron replies as himself when there is something true to say about making it.
- Respond to real comments from strangers within the day, in character when the comment is in the spirit, as Aaron when it is a real question.

## 7. Launch sequence

**Before day one**

- Banner: a still from the "Bring Your Whole Self to Work" banner installation. Synergy as the logo mark.
- About: one truthful sentence that this is a creative project with a real site, then the form line.
- Pin: "Linked Beats is now on LinkedIn. Grateful."
- Website field points to linkedinbeats.com with UTM `?utm_source=linkedin&utm_medium=page`.

**Week one.** One achievement per day, each from a different character, so the cast is introduced through voice rather than a cast list. Friday: the first clip.

**Week two.** Fold in the tool confession, the first transcript, the first credential image.

**Week three.** The humbling, the unexpected lesson, the first document post. By day fifteen every format has appeared at least once.

**Weeks four to nine.** Run the bible's connected arc as a season: First week, Access granted, Website update, Early impressions, Investing in growth, Continuing the conversation. Kevin still needs folder access in week nine. Carry the detail forward; it is what makes it one office.

## 8. Going viral: what is actually controllable

Virality is not a setting. These are the levers that move reach on LinkedIn specifically, and how this project pulls each one.

1. **Saves and dwell time.** The algorithm rewards posts people stop on and keep. Transcripts and document posts earn saves. Keep them scannable: short lines, white space, no link in the body.
2. **Comments from strangers.** Comments weigh more than reactions, and replies to comments weigh more again. Arjun's question is an invitation: readers answer it before the character does. The page reply keeps the thread alive.
3. **The shareable object.** Reshares with commentary are the biggest multiplier. Certificates are the object. A "Certificate of Exceptional Alignment" with a real name on it gets reshared by the person named. Every credential post should make a reader want one. The door is linkedinbeats.com/learn.
4. **The recognition reflex.** The most reshared satire is the kind people send to a colleague with "this is you." Each genre post should be specific enough that one real person is recognizable, and gentle enough that they laugh.
5. **The making-of layer.** LinkedIn's actual audience is people who build things. Aaron posting the real story on his own profile, with the tools, the design system, and the agent that drafts posts, is the content most likely to travel on this platform. Once a week, from the real account, resharing the page.
6. **Native everything.** Video uploaded to LinkedIn, not linked. Documents uploaded as PDF. Links go in the first comment or at the end, never in the first line.
7. **The 60 minute window.** Early engagement decides distribution. Post, reply in character within the hour, have Aaron react from his account. Do not ask friends to comment; do ask them to share if they genuinely would.
8. **One experiment per week.** Change one variable: format, slot, character, genre. Log what moved. Three weeks of logs beat any amount of theory.

What not to do: automated likes, comment pods, auto-following, engagement bots, DM blasts, or anything that touches other people's accounts programmatically. LinkedIn detects these and the penalty is the page.

## 9. Measurement

Three numbers, weekly, in a table in this folder's log:

| Metric | Why |
| --- | --- |
| Saves per post | Dwell and keep. The best signal of writing quality. |
| Comments from non-followers | Reach beyond the existing circle. |
| Clicks to linkedinbeats.com, by UTM | The only number that matters to the site. |

Impressions are reported for context only. As Arjun would note, impressions are not people.

The GA4 setup on the site already respects consent and strips query strings from the configured page location, so UTMs are visible in LinkedIn's own click counts but not in GA4 page paths. Track clicks on the LinkedIn side.

## 10. The pipeline

Three scripts, one JSON queue, two scheduled jobs. The agent drafts, a human approves, the scheduler posts. Nothing reaches LinkedIn without a status of `approved` set by a person.

```
docs/linkedin/queue.json         the content calendar and the record
scripts/linkedin/auth.mjs        one-time: get a LinkedIn access token
scripts/linkedin/publish.mjs     posts anything due and approved
scripts/linkedin/draft.mjs       Claude proposes new posts from the grid
.github/workflows/linkedin-publish.yml   hourly, runs publish
.github/workflows/linkedin-draft.yml     weekly, runs draft and opens a PR
```

**Queue item statuses**

- `draft`: proposed by the agent or a person. Not eligible to post.
- `approved`: reviewed by a person. Posts when `scheduled_at` passes.
- `published`: done. Carries the post URN and URL.
- `failed`: the API rejected it. Carries the error. Fix and set back to `approved`.
- `skipped`: deliberately not posting.

**Daily operation**

1. Monday: the drafting job opens a pull request with next week's proposals. Read them like scripts. Edit freely. Flip the good ones to `approved`, delete the rest. Merge.
2. Every hour: the publishing job checks the queue, posts anything approved and due, writes the result back, and commits.
3. Within the hour of a post: reply in character from the page, by hand.
4. Friday: fill in the log table at the bottom of this file.

**Commands**

```sh
npm run linkedin:auth               # browser flow, writes scripts/linkedin/token.local.json
npm run linkedin:publish -- --dry-run   # show what would post, post nothing
npm run linkedin:publish            # post due approved items
npm run linkedin:draft -- --count 5 # propose five new posts into the queue
npm run check:linkedin-queue        # validate the queue and the publisher offline
```

## 11. The API, honestly

The repository already contains a production LinkedIn integration in `server/linkedin.js` for member sharing. The pipeline reuses its request shapes.

**What works today, with the current developer app.** The "Share on LinkedIn" product grants `w_member_social`, which posts to a member's own profile. No review required. Aaron can post from his own profile by API right now, and that is where the making-of layer lives anyway.

**What posting as the company page requires.** The Posts API accepts an organization author only with the `w_organization_social` scope. That scope comes with the Community Management API product, which LinkedIn grants after an application review. The review expects a registered legal organization and a commercial use case, and it can take weeks. The authenticated member must also hold an admin or content admin role on the page. Until that is approved, page posts are made by hand or through LinkedIn's own scheduler, using the text the pipeline produced.

**Tokens.** Access tokens last 60 days. Self-serve apps do not get refresh tokens, so every 60 days someone runs `npm run linkedin:auth` again and updates the `LINKEDIN_ACCESS_TOKEN` secret. The publisher checks token expiry and fails loudly a week ahead instead of silently stopping.

**Limits.** 150 API requests per member per day. A post is one request; an image post is three. The pipeline will never approach this.

**Setup**

1. In the LinkedIn developer app, confirm "Share on LinkedIn" and "Sign In with LinkedIn using OpenID Connect" are added. Apply for "Community Management API" with the page attached.
2. Add `http://localhost:8787/callback` to the app's authorized redirect URLs for the local auth helper.
3. Run `npm run linkedin:auth`. It prints the person URN and writes a local token file that git ignores.
4. Add repository secrets: `LINKEDIN_ACCESS_TOKEN`, `LINKEDIN_AUTHOR_URN` (`urn:li:person:...` now, `urn:li:organization:...` once approved), `ANTHROPIC_API_KEY` for drafting.
5. Set the repository variable `LINKEDIN_PUBLISH_ENABLED=true` when you want the hourly job to actually post. Until then it runs dry.

Official references:

- Posts API: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/posts-api
- Images API: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/images-api
- Videos API: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/videos-api
- Documents API: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/marketing/community-management/shares/documents-api
- Share on LinkedIn (member scope): https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/consumer/integrations/self-serve/share-on-linkedin
- Authorization code flow: https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/linkedin/shared/authentication/authorization-code-flow

## 12. Guardrails

- No fake accounts. No engagement automation. No scraping.
- Nothing posts without a human setting `approved`.
- The About section states truthfully that this is a creative project with a real site.
- Every post and every character line lives in this repository first. If the page is removed, the show is not.
- Never send submitted user posts, names, or lyrics anywhere as analytics parameters.
- The drafting agent writes fiction about fictional employees. It never names real people or real companies other than the project's own.
