# LinkedIn content: the trend desk

The posts are not jokes about LinkedIn. They are LinkedIn posts. Each one takes a story the feed
is already arguing about and answers it the way a sincere executive would: confident, specific,
structured, and wrong in one place.

## The method

1. **Monday: read the news.** Pick three stories the professional internet is reacting to.
2. **Find the sincere take.** What would a VP post about this before 8am?
3. **Write that post, properly.** Real hook, real anecdote, real numbered lessons.
4. **Bend one beam.** A single flaw in the logic. Everything else holds.
5. **Pair it** with a film, song or tool from the site.
6. **Reveal in the first comment**, never in the post: `From the desk of LinkedIn Beats. linkedinbeats.com`

## What makes it convincing

- **Specifics.** "Four people." "Fourteen months." "$900." Vague is the tell of a parody.
- **A named colleague** who is fictional, ordinary, and slightly wiser than the author. Ours is Denise.
- **The humble pivot.** The author always learns something. It is always flattering.
- **One line worth screenshotting.** That line carries the post out of LinkedIn.
- **A closing question** people can actually answer.
- **Never** a real company, a real person, or a made-up statistic presented as research.
  The trend is real. The anecdote is ours.

## This week's slate (week of November 9, 2026)

### 1. The rehire

**Trend:** "AI rebounding." A survey reported by Fortune on November 9 says about one in five US
workers replaced by AI were later asked back, and half of them wanted a raise.
**Pair with:** Moving Things Forward (`public/cutting-room/films/moving-things-forward-7fdb2147.mp4`)
**The bent beam:** line three.

> Eight months ago I replaced my operations team with AI agents.
>
> Last Tuesday I asked all four of them to come back.
>
> It was the hardest call I have made as a leader. So I had an agent make it.
>
> Three things I learned:
>
> 1. The agents were never the problem. They did exactly what we asked. That was the problem.
>
> 2. Institutional knowledge is real. Ours was in Denise.
>
> 3. Humility has a price. Three of the four asked for a raise. I am told this is called market feedback.
>
> We are not calling it rehiring. We are calling it a return to human.
>
> Denise starts Monday. She has asked not to be tagged.
>
> What is the bravest reversal you have made this year?

### 2. The flag

**Trend:** LinkedIn's "seems like AI slop" report button (August 2026) and the wider backlash
against machine-written thought leadership.
**Pair with:** Roast My LinkedIn (`public/share/roast-characters-20261109-v1.jpg`), link `/roast` in the comment.
**The bent beam:** the last three lines. This is the brand's thesis in one post.

> Last week one of my posts was reported as AI slop.
>
> I wrote that post myself. On a plane. About my father.
>
> My first reaction was anger. My second was to draft a response. My third was to read the post again.
>
> The reader was right.
>
> I have spent eleven years learning to write like this. Short lines. A lesson by line four.
> A question at the end so you will comment.
>
> The machine did not replace my voice.
>
> It learned it. From me. From all of us.
>
> We are not being mistaken for AI. AI is being mistaken for us.
>
> Agree?

### 3. The headcount

**Trend:** executives describing a future of millions of AI agents working beside a shrinking staff.
**Pair with:** Welcome to the Office (V6 cut from the Cutting Room).
**The bent beam:** the one-to-ones.

> I have 216 direct reports.
>
> Four of them are people.
>
> When we deployed our first agents, I made a promise: everyone on this team gets the same leadership.
>
> So the agents get one-to-ones. They get quarterly reviews. They get feedback.
>
> Last quarter, one agent asked for nothing, missed nothing, and thanked me twice.
>
> I promoted it.
>
> The four humans asked why. That is a fair question, and I told them so in their reviews.
>
> Culture is not what you say in the all-hands.
> It is how you treat the ones who cannot leave.
>
> How are you leading your blended team?

### 4. The retention

**Trend:** "job hugging." Workers staying put out of caution, not loyalty.
**Pair with:** Day One. (`public/cutting-room/films/day-one-0d178d01.mp4`)
**The bent beam:** the careers page.

> Nobody has resigned from my team in fourteen months.
>
> I used to present that number at board meetings. Retention: 100%.
>
> Then I did something I should have done a year ago. I asked them why they stay.
>
> I expected to hear "the mission." I expected to hear "the people."
>
> Denise said: "Where would I go?"
>
> I have thought about that answer every day since.
>
> It is honest. It is durable. And unlike the mission, it has never once changed.
>
> We have added it to the careers page.
>
> What is really keeping your best people?

### 5. The headshot

**Trend:** LinkedIn's new trust signals, including detection of synthetic profile photos (reported November 6).
**Pair with:** the certificate from `/learn/`.
**The bent beam:** the invoice.

> LinkedIn flagged my profile photo as possibly AI-generated.
>
> It is a real photograph. I paid $900 for it.
>
> The photographer asked me to look "approachable, but acquisitive." We did 340 frames.
> A retoucher removed the tiredness. Another removed the office.
>
> I appealed. I sent the invoice.
>
> And then I looked at the photo for a long time.
>
> No person has ever looked like that. Including me. Including that day.
>
> Authenticity is not a feature you can verify.
> It is a decision you make before the retouching.
>
> I am keeping the photo. It tested well.

### 6. The result

**Trend:** reports that most executives see no measurable productivity gain from AI so far.
**Pair with:** Optimizing (`public/cutting-room/films/optimizing-pocket-mix-bd5cea53.mp4`)
**The bent beam:** the metric.

> Our AI transformation delivered a productivity gain of zero percent.
>
> I am sharing that number because nobody else will.
>
> Eighteen months. Every team. Every workflow. Zero.
>
> Here is what the number does not show:
>
> Meetings are 20% longer, because there is more to align on.
> We produce four times as many documents.
> Every one of them has an executive summary.
>
> We were measuring output. We should have been measuring momentum.
>
> By that measure, we have never moved faster.
>
> What are you measuring that you should not be?

## Running it every week

- **Three posts a week**, Tuesday to Thursday, before 8am local.
- **Post the film natively** with the text. The link goes in the first comment with the reveal.
- **Stay in character in the replies** for the first hour. "Great question. Let's take that offline."
- **One sincere post a month** from Aaron as the maker: what this is, how it is made, why.
  The parody earns the attention. The maker post is what a podcast booker reads.
- **Keep the best line.** Every screenshot-worthy line goes on a list. That list is the next film.
