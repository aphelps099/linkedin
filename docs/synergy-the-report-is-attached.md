## Sound research and direction

- DJ Jazzy Jeff describes building early records with a 909 and turntables, cutting patterned breaks and valuing the machine's swing. Use a strong, clear drum foundation and uncomplicated bass. [Primary interview, UKF](https://ukf.com/read/the-quest-was-everything-we-talked-to-dj-jazzy-jeff-about-breakbeats-and-dubplates/).
- Timbaland emphasizes restraint, a few distinctive sounds, and leaving space for the artist. Use a dry woody knock and metallic tick as rhythmic answers, with deliberate gaps rather than layers of percussion. [Primary interview with Metro Boomin, Rolling Stone](https://au.rollingstone.com/music/music-features/timbaland-metro-boomin-musicians-on-musicians-2024-69937/).
- Karriem Riggins discusses and demonstrates the Dilla approach to rhythmic placement. Use instrument-specific timing variation and swung hats against a stable pulse, rather than applying one global swing setting. [PBS Sound Field interview and demonstration](https://pbswisconsin.org/watch/sound-field/jazz-shaped-hip-hop-but-how-did-hip-hop-change-jazz-p1sy61/).

These are compositional interpretations of the research, not an audio analysis of commercial recordings. Write original melodies and drums. No samples from the referenced artists. The approved printer master remains preserved; an 84 BPM alternate soundbed is a separate comparison. Synergy's own song uses a 96 BPM, 24-bar, 60-second structure and the established synthetic voice. The robot's rigid delivery contrasts with the human timing of the drums. No imitation of a real performer's voice.
