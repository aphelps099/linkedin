# Some Personal News.

**A LinkedIn Beats employer-brand film. 7 minutes. Dead serious.**
Form LB-FILM-01 · Rev. 2026-10 · Treatment, cut list, and media plan

---

## 1. The idea

Kyle M's *The Real Me* works because it never winks. A comedian releases a sincere
record, walks through his hometown record store, and promises "this is art that is
earnestly me." The audience does the laughing. He does the believing.

The LinkedIn version of that is not a music video. It is the **employer-brand
culture film**: the genre where a company films its own office, interviews its own
people, and tells you how humbled it is. That genre already exists on LinkedIn in
total sincerity. We make one for a band that only speaks LinkedIn.

The frame: **LinkedIn Beats has recorded a debut album.** This is the film the
company made to announce it. Everyone in it is a lunatic. Nobody in it knows.

Working title: **Some Personal News.**
Alternate titles, same film: *Humbled.* · *The Authentic Us.* · *A Journey.*

### Rules of the room

1. **No wink.** No lower-thirds that explain the joke. No laugh. No "lol" captions.
   The joke is the gap between the scale of the ceremony and the size of the news.
2. **The news is always tiny.** A folder. A dropdown. A meeting invite. An album.
3. **Every sentence is maximal.** Nobody says "we made a song." They say they are
   humbled and honored to share the next chapter of their sonic journey.
4. **The printer is a musician.** It never speaks. It is the most reliable member of
   the band.
5. **Synergy believes.** He is the Kyle M of this film. The close-up at the end has
   to land as genuinely, uncomfortably sincere.

## 3. The film: beat sheet (7:00 target, 5:30 floor, 8:30 ceiling)

Format: 16:9 master at 1080p. Source footage is 720p and 1080p; the 720p scenes
get a conservative upscale before vertical recuts (see §6).

### Cold open · 0:00–0:30 · "The announcement"

- Black. The sound of a printer warming up. One sheet feeds out. It reads:
  **SOME PERSONAL NEWS.**
- Cut to the VHS cameraman applause take. The office is clapping at Synergy, who
  is holding nothing.
- Synergy, to camera, in his approved voice: *"I'm humbled and honored to share
  that we have recorded an album."*
- Beat. *"It's eleven songs. It's under twenty minutes. It changed us."*
- Title card in the LinkedIn composer, typed live: *Some personal news.* Cursor
  blinks. Post button. Title: **LinkedIn Beats / A Culture Film**.

Why it works in two seconds without sound: robot, LinkedIn banner, applause, a
sheet of paper that says the title.

### Act 2 · 2:15–4:00 · "The process"

- Synergy at the keyboard, four coffee cups. He types into ChatGPT: *"Turn this
  into a LinkedIn post: I booked a meeting."* The reply appears word by word:
  *"Humbled and honored to unlock a new era of alignment."* He clicks Copy.
  Copied. He nods. That's the lyric.
- Interview, Synergy: *"People ask where the songs come from. I ask the same
  question. Then I ask it again, with context."*

### Act 3 · 4:00–5:45 · "The release"

- The single: *Let's Circle Back*, the corrected 90s edit cut to 40 seconds. Full

This is the Kyle M beat. Single close-up. Synergy at the caffeinated desk, cups

> *I want to be really transparent.*
> *This is the most authentic thing we have ever done.*
> *We brought our whole selves to work. We left them there.*

- End card, white on cobalt, the lb_ mark: **LinkedIn Beats. Sound like LinkedIn.**
- Post-credit: Pat's tile. *"Connecting to audio…"* Still.

## 7. Media strategy: how this reaches a media person

### The positioning, in one line for a stranger

*A designer built a fake record label where every song is a LinkedIn post, then
made the label's culture film.* That line is the pitch to press. "Another LinkedIn
parody" is not. The institution is the story: the studio, the store, the museum,
the certificate, the album. The film is the trailer for the institution.

### Three audiences, three doors

**1. The cringe economy (r/LinkedInLunatics, 1M+ members, and the creators who
feed on it).** They want new source material. Give them the clips, not the film.
Post the "found" employer-brand trailer with a straight title: *"Company releases
culture film to announce their band's album."* Disclose it's parody in the first
comment, in voice. Reddit punishes self-promo and rewards craft. Lead with the
printer.

**2. Corporate-comedy creators.** The people who already hold this audience:
Lisa Beasley (Corporate Erin), Corporate Natalie, Ben Sweeny (Corporate Sween),
Laura Whaley, Dustin Ballard (There I Ruined It, who did "LinkedIn Park"), Kyle
Gordon, and Dan Toomey (Morning Brew's *Good Work*). Do not ask them to share.
Give them a part:

- **The Roast.** Run their most-viewed post through Roast My LinkedIn and send
  the receipt. Savage register, McKinsey Partner persona.
- **The Printer.** A 10-second clip of the LinkedIn printer printing *their*
  post, with applause. Personalized, cheap to make from the existing feed take,
  impossible not to post.
- **The Remix.** Their post performed by the studio voice over a groove, exported
  from the real tool. "We turned your post into a single."
- **The Cap.** A physical THOUGHT LEADER cap and a printed Certificate of
  Professional Effort with their name on it. The store is already wired to
  Printify.

One creator stitching one clip beats fifty cold DMs. Pick three, do all four
things for each.

**3. The press that covers the genre.** Bloomberg, The Hustle, Morning Brew,
Fast Company and Dallas Morning News have all written the "LinkedIn is cringe"
piece. The next one needs a new angle: *the cringe has a record label now.* Pitch
with: the film, the album on streaming, the store, and one stat from the site
(certificates issued, posts LinkedInified). Add design press as a second lane,
since the craft is real: It's Nice That, Creative Boom, Fast Company Design,
Brand New. Lead for them: the identity, the Swiss-bureaucracy system, the
typography, the robots.

### The release, as a sequence (three weeks)

**Week 0, "Pre-onboarding."** Album to streaming via a distributor (DistroKid or
similar). Create the YouTube premiere. Load the twelve clips. Write the
personalized creator packages. Newsletter draft in voice: *"Some personal news."*

**Week 1, "The announcement."**
- Monday: the trailer, LinkedIn native video, from Aaron's profile and the
  LinkedIn Beats page. The post is itself a lunatic post: *"Humbled and honored
  to share that our office band has recorded an album. Here's what it taught us
  about B2B sales."* No link in the body. Link in the first comment.
- Tuesday: the full film premieres on YouTube. Reddit post the same morning, the
  printer clip, with the film linked in the comments.
- Wednesday to Friday: one vertical clip a day on Reels, Shorts and TikTok. Same
  clip everywhere, same day.
- Friday: creator packages go out. Roast receipt first, printer clip second.

**Week 2, "The journey."**
- Cast quote stills on LinkedIn, one a day.
- Second wave of verticals.
- Press pitches go out Tuesday, after there is a week of public numbers to cite.
- The newsletter ships: the film, the album, the store.

**Week 3, "Let's circle back."**
- Behind-the-scenes post for the design audience: the system, the robots, the
  Cutting Room, the credits ledger. This is the post that gets the design press.
- Repost the film with a Wrapped-style card: views, reactions, "times we said
  circle back." The site can draw the card.
- Open the Museum's Redaction Office to submissions from the comments.

### What counts as working

- One creator with a real audience stitches, duets or reposts a clip.
- One newsletter or outlet covers the institution, not just the clip.
- The film clears 10,000 views on LinkedIn native in week one.
- Site sessions, newsletter opt-ins and store orders all move in the same week.
- Someone on r/LinkedInLunatics posts it without us.

### Guardrails

- No fake LinkedIn profiles for the cast. Every real account involved is Aaron's
  or the company page. The cast lives in the film and on the site.
- Every target creator's post used in a personalized clip is public, credited,
  and sent to them first. Nothing goes out publicly with their content until
  they engage.
- The film stays a parody of a genre, not of a person. The Museum's rule
  applies: the genre goes on the wall, the person does not.
