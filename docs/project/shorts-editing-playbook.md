## 2. Our editing vocabulary

Start each 30–40 second film with **one recognizable problem, one principal strange beat, and one callback or consequence**. These are editorial constraints to sharpen the first experiments, not permanent limits.

**The invasive close-up.** Strategy says “single.” Hard cut much too close to his entirely sincere eyes. Hold through the word and a fraction beyond it. Return to Linda's ordinary framing: “There is one file.” A decisive cut is usually stronger here than a wandering animated zoom. Use the sharpest original source; if the crop fails at delivery resolution, acquire a proper close-up. Upscaling does not recover missing expression.

**Coffee as punctuation.** Synergy requests access, then takes a conspicuously unhurried sip while everyone discusses ownership. Let the room sound and cup make the pause audible. Later, return to him still holding the cup. The joke is the timing and his priorities. Use the supplied coffee performances; do not recycle the identical sip in every episode or animate his mouth while he drinks.

**Sound suggests enormous stakes.** A permission click, printer roller or cup landing can briefly occupy the foreground. A short music dropout makes one cough or sentence unexpectedly exposed. Keep the original musical identity and intelligible dialogue. Avoid a sound-effect announcement after every joke.

**One breach, immediate normality.** Strategy's sudden “HUMAN OVERSIGHT!” can work because everyone else continues the meeting. Linda's cough interrupts official certainty; it should not make her age the joke. An outburst earns its place through the scene, not merely a volume increase.

**The machine completes the contradiction.** Linda says not to make another copy. The printer makes one. Nobody looks at it. Give the paper enough screen time to register before the cut.

**Hold a face with a reason.** A short silence should expose an unanswered question, impending approval or someone choosing not to respond. Start by testing roughly half a second; extend only when the tension grows. Constant movement and arbitrary waiting both weaken the contrast.

Weirdness belongs in framing, timing and consequences. Keep faces, printer mechanics, camera geometry and character identities consistent. Avoid zigzag pans, random glitches and distorted bodies.

## 4. Package it for the actual feed

The current 1920×1080 films are landscape. Short duration alone does not make them YouTube Shorts: qualifying uploads are square or vertical and up to three minutes. Create a separate **1080×1920 version** while retaining the website master. [YouTube eligibility](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/15424877?hl=en)

- Recompose around one large speaker and, when useful, a smaller listener. Use the full gallery briefly for context. Do not shrink an entire desktop meeting into the middle of a vertical frame.
- Preserve charcoal meeting UI, name bars, green speaker indication and equal camera geometry. The camera image inside each tile remains 4:3 with pillarboxing where required; the overall Short is 9:16.
- Keep approved paper/graph-paper collage, TT Hoves and actual band imagery. For Shorts, test a brief brand accent after the hook or over the action. This proposed variant does not replace the approved 2.5-second intro in existing website films.
- Captions must be readable on a phone and avoid app controls. Preview in the upload UI. Highlight an occasional meaningful word, not every syllable; no microtext or stacked duplicate labels.
- Keep the music audible but subordinate to speech. Preserve the original Synergy voice. Use silence and ordinary machine sounds selectively.
- Synergy's mouth stays one green 1×10 row, filling left to right, with a 10% idle row and fixed height approximately half his 2×2 eyes. Comedy never overrides that geometry.
- Printer sheets feed forward at realistic scale. Coffee clips must retain normal hands, cup and screen behavior. Inspect each chosen take, rather than assuming the entire supplied library is clean.

Thumbnail art still matters to the channel's identity. The opening moving image also needs its own composition; an excellent thumbnail cannot repair an unclear first exchange.

## 5. A small repeatable production method

Before editing, complete: **“Someone will send this to ___ because ___.”** For this episode: a coworker, because their team also turns a file-access problem into a governance initiative.

Then choose one visual contradiction and build the short around it. Mark the extreme close-up, coffee pause and consequence in the shot list. Reuse existing performances first; commission new media only for a specific missing action or sharper crop. Review once without sound for visual clarity, once audio-only for dialogue and rhythm, then on a phone with captions.

Next three experiments:

1. **One source:** close-up on “single,” coffee during approval, printer duplicate.
2. **Thoroughly reviewed:** sudden Strategy outburst, Linda's cough, Corporate continuing with absolute composure. Keep the reactions separated so each can register.
3. **Rollout ready:** the playbook for adopting the playbook needs its own approval. Synergy takes a sip precisely when a practical answer is expected; end on the new approval request.

Do not put every device in every episode. Repetition should establish character, with a different consequence each time.

## 6. Learn from the first uploads

Log the first 6–10 Shorts as a starting baseline. Review at 24 hours and seven days—our working windows, not platform rules. Compare similar lengths and topics. Change one major editorial choice across a matched pair where possible; different audiences and premises still prevent a clean causal claim.

Track **chose to view versus swiped away, average view duration/percentage, shares per 1,000 engaged views, and subscribers gained**. Read comments for recognition, quoted lines and confusion. Shares per 1,000 engaged views is our comparison ratio, not a YouTube ranking formula or unique-person sharing probability. A retention spike may reflect a satisfying rewatch or something hard to understand.

YouTube's Shorts analytics supports comparing like formats and normal channel performance. Since March 31, 2025, Shorts views include playback/replay starts without a minimum watch time; use the retained engaged-views metric for a more meaningful comparison of people choosing to continue. [Analytics guidance](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/12942217?co=YOUTUBE._YTVideoType%3Dshorts&hl=en) · [Views and engaged views](https://support.google.com/youtube/answer/10059070?hl=en)

If people leave before the conflict, repair the opening. If they watch but rarely share, sharpen the recognizable situation and payoff. If comments ask what happened, clarify the visual cause and effect before adding another flourish.

**Release question:** did the camera discover something strange about this otherwise completely serious meeting?
