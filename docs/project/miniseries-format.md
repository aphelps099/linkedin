## Series format

Episodes run 90 seconds to 4 minutes. A 2–3 second branded opening shows the actual LinkedIn Beats logo and episode title, then goes directly into a specific workplace problem. Use a small collection of storybook illustrations, spoken performances, music, precisely timed words and restrained paper animation.

The characters believe the stakes. Realistic achievements stay centered on onboarding, Google Drive sharing, website updates, a social media intern’s post impressions, professional-development time and conference follow-up. The recurring “I used ChatGPT to…” framing belongs here when it supports a story. Characters review and apply the output; the script should distinguish drafting advice from actually changing files or permissions.

The medium is economical, but the writing and sound need full attention. Each episode needs an objective, an obstacle, a change and a consequence. Do not simply stretch a caption or jargon list to four minutes.

## Picture vocabulary

Use hand-cut ivory/manila and blue memo paper, carbon-black contours, soft copier grain and shallow layer shadows. Preserve the established cast faces, proportions, wardrobe cues and robot geometry.

Start a pilot with about 4–6 source illustrations, plus reusable text plates and character states. This is a planning target, not a limit. A wide meeting grid, a speaker close-up and a silent reaction can come from the same layered set. Change framing or emphasis when the conversation changes; avoid an unchanged picture under a long monologue.

Three recurring picture modes:

- **Office scene:** desk, printer station or corridor, with one meaningful action.
- **Paper meeting:** individual camera windows mounted as rounded paper cards with equal border widths.
- **Text stage:** plain ivory or cobalt textured paper carrying a short phrase in TT Hoves, timed to the voice.

Keep actual logo and type as separate editable assets. Do not generate finished lettering inside scene images. On blue, use ivory lettering; on ivory, navy. An occasional flat highlight can carry the stressed phrase. No microtext, dense paragraphs or constant bouncing words. Preserve pauses and let some lines play without onscreen repetition. Provide ordinary captions independently of the expressive text.

## Meetings as the recurring stage

A participant joins by sliding a paper window into place with one short shuffle and settle. A cut to a speaker can enlarge or crop that window; a quiet edge emphasis can identify the active speaker. A rearrangement should follow a change in the conversation, rather than happen continuously.

Keep camera positions, eye lines and character scale stable. Use a neutral listening state, a speaking state and one restrained reaction per character initially. Printed-paper texture stays attached to the asset rather than crawling between frames. Build rounded masks and equal borders in the compositor.

[Paper meeting concept](art/paper-meeting-grid-v1.png) tests Synergy, People & Culture, Operations and the printer as four participants. It is a flattened still concept, not a delivered animation rig. The printer’s participation in the grid is a new staging proposal.

## Printer and coffee

The printer is an important recurring presence, not occasional set dressing. Suggest tiredness through a hesitant feed, a slightly longer motor cycle, a lone amber light, wear on its housing or a pause before the last page. The joke works without giving it a cartoon face.

Maintain ordinary sheet dimensions, forward feed and consistent stacks. No growing sheets, reversed output or resetting stacks in continuous action. Use separate paper layers and simple controlled motion for printing.

Synergy’s coffee is a recurring quiet action: hold, raise, pause, lower. Keep his angular head, pixel eyes, straight mouth line and mechanical joints consistent. Do not add human lips or fluid human gestures. A cup held during an awkward silence may work better than a literal sip. Separate cup and hand states make the action reusable.

The floor droid can move a paper trolley between scenes. Preserve the towbar and wheels; no rotating cloth or duster.

## Sound carries the performance

Record and edit dialogue before animating words. Keep voices clear and close, with restrained robot processing, minimal echo and deliberate pauses. Preserve existing cast identities. The printer remains silent in the established meeting engine; its emotional presence comes through sound and timing.

Use original rhythmic beds with space for speech, separate drum/bass/harmonic stems, and short robotic beep replies. A little motor or paper percussion can connect the score to the room. Duck music under important lines. Recurring motifs should identify the world without making every episode the same song.

## Reusable production kit

Build a title template, ivory and blue text plates, the paper meeting layout, office/printer sets, and a small collection of cast cutouts and prop states. Store clean foreground/background layers, masks, character anchor points, typography settings, audio stems and timing data. Keep original flat studies alongside production assets.

Pipeline: script and shot list → cast voice takes → edited audio → word/phrase timing → stills and layered assets → controlled local compositing → review → export. Local rendering can handle cuts, pans, crops, paper slides, highlights and simple prop motion. Use generated video only where a shot actually benefits from it.

Check likeness, legibility on mobile, clear speech, equal borders, stable textures, paper continuity, audio/text sync and scene pacing. Save manifests, prompts and approval notes in the repo; keep large masters and stems in managed media storage.

**Yes, this can use substantially fewer Runway video-generation credits.** Runway charges video generation by duration and model. A locally composited still held for ten seconds does not require ten seconds of generated video.
