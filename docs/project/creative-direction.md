# LinkedIn Beats creative direction

## Writing and performance

Corporate sincerity is the default. The absurdity is the gap between the language, the resources committed and the actual outcome. Never tell the audience that a scene is absurd. Use patient listening, precise responses and ordinary office detail. Avoid reaction faces, wink-to-camera deliveries, punchline sound effects and a parade of interchangeable buzzwords.

A scene needs an objective, an obstacle, an escalation and an institutional resolution. The resolution may leave the practical problem untouched. Language should reveal what the speaker wants: reassurance, status, clarity, access, credit or permission.

Example development scene:

> Kevin: “I still can’t open the folder.”
>
> People & Culture: “You’ve been added to the welcome channel.”
>
> Arjun: “Does the channel contain the folder?”
>
> Synergy: “Let’s keep the two workstreams distinct.”

The humor is carried by the process and the delivery. Do not add a caption explaining it.

## Standing rule for achievements

User rule, October 29, 2026: achievements should be realistic and centered on onboarding, Google Drive sharing, website updates, a social media intern getting impressions on posts, time spent on professional development, and conference follow-up. This governs new scripts, lyrics, ChatGPT screen prompts, certificate concepts and personalized meeting stories.

Start with a credible piece of work and its actual outcome. The satire comes from the announcement, self-reflection, process and importance attached to it. Keep the accomplishment plausible even when described without corporate language. Avoid defaulting to a folder rename or similarly empty action as the whole achievement.

| Focus | Credible achievement | Possible announcement language |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Onboarding | Completed the new-hire checklist and helped a colleague find the team's resources | “Reflecting on a first week built around connection, clarity and shared purpose.” |
| Google Drive sharing | Organized onboarding files, corrected permissions and shared the right link with the team | “Making knowledge more accessible across the organization.” |
| Website updates | Published revised service information and fixed an outdated registration link | “A small update to how we connect people with the resources they need.” |
| Social media intern | Published a campaign post, checked its impressions and reported what they learned | “Encouraged by the visibility our latest post is generating.” |
| Professional development | Spent an afternoon in a webinar or completed a short training module | “Making space to invest in the next stage of my professional growth.” |
| Conference follow-up | Sent promised materials and follow-up emails to people met at an event | “Continuing the conversations that made this week's gathering so valuable.” |

These are fictional writing examples, not assertions about real results. If a user supplies numbers, preserve what they mean: impressions are not unique people, clicks, leads or revenue; time in training is not automatically mastery; follow-up is not a completed partnership. Do not invent impact to make the post more impressive.

The intern's work can be genuinely useful. Aim the humor at inflated corporate framing and the pressure to announce progress, not at a junior person's competence. All characters remain sincere.

## Recurring ChatGPT announcement format

User addition, October 29, 2026: “I used ChatGPT to optimize the…” and “I used ChatGPT to…” are recurring openings. Apply this format across the six realistic workplace topics. Give the tool a specific role in drafting, organizing, summarizing or reviewing; the character still checks and applies the result.

Possible lines:

- “I used ChatGPT to optimize our onboarding checklist.”
- “I used ChatGPT to draft a guide to our shared Google Drive.”
- “I used ChatGPT to refine the copy on our registration page.”
- “I used ChatGPT to write three captions for our next campaign post.”
- “I used ChatGPT to turn my webinar notes into an action plan.”
- “I used ChatGPT to personalize my conference follow-up emails.”

Develop each into a sincere professional reflection. For example: “I used ChatGPT to optimize our onboarding checklist. We combined two steps and clarified who grants access. A small change, but an important reminder of what becomes possible when we rethink how we work.” The practical outcome is credible; the inflated significance and announcement ritual carry the satire.

Use the format as a recurring motif, not the opening of every scene. Keep outcomes specific and modest. Drafting captions does not by itself establish increased impressions. A guide to sharing does not mean ChatGPT changed Drive permissions. The character using ChatGPT and the character asking it to write the announcement can be the same person; keep that additional layer understated.

## Music and synth

Build original songs with real rhythmic weight: a clear kick/snare foundation, bass with purpose, sparse keys or guitar, and space for the voice. Character-specific timing and sounds matter more than adding layers. Robotic precision can contrast with an uneven, human drum pocket. Keep beeps brief and meaningful, with gaps around speech.

Save drums, bass, harmony, instruments, lead voice, responses and beeps separately. Record tempo, key, bar structure, phrase timings, loudness, source and rights. Keep the voice intelligible on phone speakers as well as headphones; avoid excess reverb or hollow robot processing. A measured gain change is not a substitute for listening.

The synth draws from approved clean voice takes and original music. Musical edits must not silently change a character’s voice. Maintain compatible sound IDs for saved projects. Develop three reusable bed families first: restrained hip-hop, clipped office funk and mechanical/electronic process music. These are proposed production families, not promises of new shipped presets.

## Thumbnail art

The [approved art notes](../thumbnail-art-notes.md) remain the detailed authority. The essential rules are:

- Art itself has rounded corners above a separate white caption body.
- Frames have equal width on all four edges, with concentric inner and outer corners.
- Use real cast references, dimensional lighting, deliberate crops and varied compositions.
- No microtext. Usually no words or one short line; TT Hoves remains an editable type layer where possible.
- Selective highlighted text, meeting tiles, active-speaker outlines and small video symbols can work; avoid repetitive full Zoom interfaces.
- Avoid the overly polished orange/chrome camera-object direction. Protect the colorful, tactile character of the site.
- Share belongs in the media player, not on carousel artwork or caption cards.

Develop several composition families: a framed close portrait; a meeting grid; a cutout interacting with a rounded aperture; a controlled object study; and a photographic scene with one subtle graphic intervention. The concept determines the treatment.

## Roast and certificates

Treat Roast as a professionally delivered assessment. The user’s wording supplies the material; the output should recognize actual habits rather than return generic insults. Keep the useful diagnosis legible beneath the corporate ceremony.

Learn certificates already exist. A dedicated Roast certificate is a proposed extension, with its own truthfulness rules: do not imply course completion or professional accreditation. Possible artifact titles include “Certificate of Exceptional Alignment” and “Professional Use of the Word Humbled.” These are creative options, not finalized labels.

A Roast certificate should contain a name or alias, a short finding grounded in the submitted post, one restrained credential line and a shareable result. Preview before sharing; do not expose the full submitted post by default. Use the same editable typography and consistent certificate identity. The ceremonial presentation can be elaborate while the claim remains very small.

## Review standard

Judge the work in context: a thumbnail in the carousel, a face at webcam size, a vocal on a phone and a scene at normal playback speed. Review paper feeds, wheels, hands, instruments and identity frame by frame around transitions. Preserve approved masters and identify revisions. Publishing a new render does not make it canon; the project’s character and art records must identify the approved version.
