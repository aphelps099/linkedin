# LinkedIn Beats — recurring story engine

Development direction approved by Aaron on October 29, 2026. Specific episode premises and sample dialogue below are writing drafts, not released episodes.

## What keeps these people in the same room

Synergy wants every small improvement to mean something larger. Operations wants it to work. People & Culture wants the actual person's experience to match the announcement.

Synergy genuinely believes that clearer processes and a shared vocabulary can create a unified company. He does useful work. His blind spot is treating a persuasive description of connection as proof that connection happened. He also wants to belong: a sincere human response matters more to him than he knows how to admit.

Operations likes finishing things and takes pride in practical competence. The flaw is impatience: finishing a task does not always mean another person understands it or feels included. People & Culture notices that gap, but can overcomplicate a simple kindness by trying to make it repeatable.

These are competing needs, not a clever character paired with a foolish one. The recurring question: **Will Synergy improve the company, or improve its account of itself? Sometimes both.**

## How an episode moves

1. Start with a real, modest objective: grant access, update a page, reply to someone, finish a course.
2. Synergy makes a useful contribution and attaches a larger ambition to it.
3. Another person's ordinary need complicates the success story. Show the obstacle before explaining it.
4. A practical attempt changes the situation. Let someone other than the usual fixer help.
5. Complete one small thing. Keep its cost visible: time, attention, another person's labor, one more printed revision.
6. Let the after-meeting behavior reveal the gap between the event and its public account. Sometimes Synergy surprises us by choosing the person over the post.

For 90 seconds, one obstacle and one turn are enough. For four minutes, add a second attempt that changes the relationship, not more jargon. Silence, a cursor and a listening face can carry a turn.

## Performance and visual rules

Nobody knows they are in a satire. No eye-roll chorus or repeated sarcastic corrections. Hold the sincerity of a modest statement. Use a close-up when someone's confidence changes, not simply because they speak.

The printer supplies physical evidence: three versions of a one-page simplification, an access instruction sheet, a printed networking list. Paper moves forward at ordinary size. It need not provide a joke in every episode.

The band provides a second way of being together. A brief unplanned musical response may achieve the connection that a culture initiative could not. Keep instruments tied to the established cast: Keys on keyboard, Kevin on guitar, Operations on drums. Synergy can listen before trying to organize it.

Intro: preserve the approved 2.5-second graph-paper/blue-paper collage, TT Hoves, actual logo and title for the established website films. The [Shorts playbook](shorts-editing-playbook.md) proposes a separate cold-open/brief-brand-accent experiment for vertical edits; it has not replaced the approved master. Add short photographic performance scraps on successive beats of the original 96 BPM bed: keys at 0.625, guitar at 1.25, drums at 1.875. Quick arrivals, then steady paper. Do not speed up the performers or require the generic score to match a particular visible note.

Outro: preserve the approved collage hashtag ending and small lower-corner wordmark/URL. Allow the final hashtags to acquire meaning from the story rather than explicitly explain its joke.

## Next script candidate — Great connecting

Target: about 100 seconds. Reuse the established meeting stage, a printer insert and a short desktop action. No new character or voice is required. This is a beat sheet for the next script pass, not a request to generate media yet.

- **0–2.5:** approved branded collage; title “Great connecting.” Instrument scraps on the beat.
- **2.5–16:** Kevin asks for the contact who offered to introduce a venue. Synergy has prepared conference follow-ups. A tangible need is already present.
- **16–34:** Synergy explains that every message includes a personalized observation. People asks which conversation he remembers. He gives a sincere but unspecific answer. Hold the pause.
- **34–51:** One recipient has replied: “Good to meet you too. Did you find someone for the Friday session?” Kevin recognizes the actual problem. Operations asks whether they can help.
- **51–72:** Synergy searches his notes rather than drafting a reflection. The useful contact is in an unremarkable note Kevin kept. Kevin offers to make the introduction. Synergy asks before sharing the contact's details.
- **72–86:** They send a short, specific response in the story. No triumphant announcement. The printer finishes the beautifully formatted follow-up plan they no longer need.
- **86–95:** Synergy starts “A reminder that meaningful connections…” in a draft, pauses, then returns to the actual conversation. Let the action be visible rather than narrated.
- **95–100:** hashtag collage: #MeaningfulConnections / #FollowingUp. Small brand mark and URL. Keep the original voice and musical palette.

Possible dialogue anchors, to test in the full script:

> People & Culture: “What did you talk about?”
>
> Synergy: “The importance of staying connected.”

That exchange changes the available solution. The joke also reveals that this company already contains something useful that its shared vocabulary failed to describe.
