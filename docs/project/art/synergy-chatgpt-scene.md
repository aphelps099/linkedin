## Recreating the style

Still scenes are feasible with approved identity references, a small texture palette and explicit composition briefs. New generations can drift in head proportions, hands, paper edges or layout, so references and review remain necessary. One successful still is not proof of frame-to-frame animation consistency.

For repeatable animated scenes, develop a reusable layered asset kit: Synergy front/three-quarter/profile heads; fixed screen face; headset and lanyard; separate torso, upper/lower arms and hands; coffee cup, keyboard, monitor and cubicle components; and a controlled set of manila, memo, carbon and copier textures. Keep the texture attached to each part so it does not shimmer between frames.

Build performances from limited deliberate motion: two or three typing poses, small head inclinations, fixed-height green mouth animation and brief screen changes. Keep the mouth neutral during silent typing. Animate screen copy as editable type in production, not newly generated lettering each frame. Preserve strong silhouettes and consistent relative scale. Wider camera moves and new angles need additional art rather than stretching a single flat image.

The present deliverable is a flattened concept still. Layer separation, model sheets, rigging, typing motion and a short continuity test are the next production work. The final physical monitor/camera arrangement must support both character sightline and legible screen inserts; this concept favors audience readability.
